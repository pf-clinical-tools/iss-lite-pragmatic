ISS-Lite (Pragmatic) Calculator

Bedside approximation of injury burden using simplified severity bands (0–5).
Not a substitute for formal AIS/ISS coding or trauma registry methodology.
Important: For usability, the spine is shown as separate cervical/thoracic/lumbar selections.
However, ISS-Lite still behaves as a 6-region system (not 9).

How spine is handled in ISS-Lite (read this)

ISS-Lite still behaves like a 6-region system, not 9. The additional spine dropdowns are provided for usability only.

Important governance note:
Spinal regions are separated for usability. For ISS-Lite calculation, cervical spine is grouped with Head/Neck, and thoracic/lumbar spine are grouped with Extremities, consistent with AIS regional principles. The highest severity within each grouped region is used.

Grouping rule used by the calculator:
  • Head/Neck group = max(Head/Brain, Cervical spine)
  • Extremities group = max(Extremities/Pelvic girdle, Thoracic spine, Lumbar spine)
All other regions (Face, Chest, Abdomen/Pelvic contents, External) are unchanged.
What qualifies in Head/Brain? (0–5)

0 – No injury

  • No traumatic intracranial injury.

1 – Minor

  • Superficial scalp injury only; no intracranial injury.

2 – Moderate

  • Definite head injury without intracranial haemorrhage and without significant neurological risk.

3 – Serious

  • Any intracranial haemorrhage (SDH/EDH/SAH/ICH) without mass effect or midline shift.
  • Injury requiring inpatient observation/active management.

4 – Severe

  • Mass effect, significant cerebral oedema, SDH ≥10 mm, or midline shift ≥5 mm.

5 – Critical

  • Herniation/brainstem compression or emergency neurosurgical decompression.
Tip: a “thin SDH” (e.g., 2–4 mm) with no shift is usually 3.
Uncertainty rule: if unsure between two categories, choose the higher band or escalate.
What qualifies in Face? (0–5)

0 – No injury

  • No facial injury.

1 – Minor

  • Superficial facial injury; uncomplicated nasal fracture without displacement.

2 – Moderate

  • Uncomplicated facial fractures requiring routine care but no major threat.

3 – Serious

  • Complex facial fractures requiring specialist input (e.g., operative planning) but no immediate airway threat.

4 – Severe

  • Facial trauma with significant haemorrhage risk or high complexity, potential airway concern.

5 – Critical

  • Airway threatened by facial trauma, or immediately life-threatening facial injury.
Tip: if the dominant problem is airway/physiology, escalate — this tool does not capture physiology.
What qualifies in Cervical Spine? (0–5)
Grouping note: For ISS-Lite calculation, Cervical spine is grouped with Head/Neck and the higher of (Head/Brain, Cervical spine) is used.

0 – No injury

  • No cervical spine injury.

1 – Minor

  • Minor soft-tissue injury only (e.g., uncomplicated neck strain).

2 – Moderate

  • Example: stable cervical vertebral fracture without neurological deficit.

3 – Serious

  • Example: unstable cervical fracture/ligamentous injury requiring specialist input/immobilisation, without imminent life-threatening compromise.

4 – Severe

  • Example: cervical injury with neurological deficit, significant canal compromise, or high risk of deterioration.

5 – Critical

  • Example: high cervical cord injury threatening ventilation or requiring immediate life-saving intervention.
Uncertainty rule: if unsure, choose the higher band or escalate.
What qualifies in Chest? (0–5)

0 – No injury

  • No thoracic injury.

1 – Minor

  • Bruising/superficial injury only; no rib fracture/pleural injury.

2 – Moderate

  • Single uncomplicated rib fracture; small contusion without respiratory compromise.

3 – Serious

  • ≥2 rib fractures with contusion; pneumothorax requiring observation or drainage.
  • Injury requiring inpatient care and active management.

4 – Severe

  • Flail chest; massive haemothorax; pulmonary contusion with respiratory failure.

5 – Critical

  • Immediately life-threatening thoracic injury (e.g., major thoracic vascular injury).
Uncertainty rule: if unsure, choose the higher band or escalate.
What qualifies in Thoracic Spine? (0–5)
Grouping note: For ISS-Lite calculation, Thoracic spine is grouped with Extremities and the highest of (Extremities, Thoracic spine, Lumbar spine) is used.

0 – No injury

  • No thoracic spine injury.

1 – Minor

  • Minor soft-tissue injury only.

2 – Moderate

  • Example: stable thoracic compression fracture without neurological deficit.

3 – Serious

  • Example: burst fracture or unstable injury requiring specialist input/brace/surgical planning, without severe neurological compromise.

4 – Severe

  • Example: thoracic injury with neurological deficit, significant canal compromise, or high risk of deterioration.

5 – Critical

  • Example: profound spinal neurological compromise requiring urgent intervention.
Uncertainty rule: if unsure, choose the higher band or escalate.
What qualifies in Abdomen/Pelvic Contents? (0–5)

0 – No injury

  • No abdominal/pelvic visceral injury.

1 – Minor

  • Abdominal wall bruising only; no visceral injury.

2 – Moderate

  • Definite injury but generally not life-threatening (e.g., minor stable solid-organ injury without concerning features).

3 – Serious

  • Solid organ injury without haemodynamic instability; pelvic bleeding requiring active management.

4 – Severe

  • Major intra-abdominal haemorrhage; unstable pelvic injury with significant bleeding risk.

5 – Critical

  • Exsanguinating abdominal/pelvic injury or imminent death without intervention.
Uncertainty rule: if unsure, choose the higher band or escalate.
What qualifies in Lumbar Spine? (0–5)
Grouping note: For ISS-Lite calculation, Lumbar spine is grouped with Extremities and the highest of (Extremities, Thoracic spine, Lumbar spine) is used.

0 – No injury

  • No lumbar spine injury.

1 – Minor

  • Minor soft-tissue injury only.

2 – Moderate

  • Example: stable lumbar compression fracture without neurological deficit.

3 – Serious

  • Example: unstable lumbar injury requiring specialist input/brace/surgical planning, without severe neurological compromise.

4 – Severe

  • Example: lumbar injury with neurological deficit, significant canal compromise, or high risk of deterioration.

5 – Critical

  • Example: cauda equina syndrome or profound neurological compromise requiring urgent intervention.
Uncertainty rule: if unsure, choose the higher band or escalate.
What qualifies in Extremities/Pelvic Girdle? (0–5)
Grouping note: For ISS-Lite calculation, Extremities is grouped with Thoracic/Lumbar spine and the highest of (Extremities, Thoracic spine, Lumbar spine) is used.

0 – No injury

  • No extremity/pelvic girdle injury.

1 – Minor

  • Sprain/bruise; superficial injury only.

2 – Moderate

  • Closed limb fracture not requiring urgent surgery; stable injuries with low immediate threat.

3 – Serious

  • Hip fracture; femoral shaft fracture; open fracture without vascular compromise.

4 – Severe

  • Limb-threatening injury; crush injury with compartment syndrome.

5 – Critical

  • Traumatic amputation (especially with shock) or immediately limb-/life-threatening extremity injury.
Uncertainty rule: if unsure, choose the higher band or escalate.
What qualifies in External? (0–5)

0 – No injury

  • No external injury.

1 – Minor

  • Superficial skin injury only (abrasions/contusions) with minimal clinical impact.

2 – Moderate

  • More extensive soft-tissue injury requiring treatment/monitoring but not typically life-threatening.

3 – Serious

  • Significant burns or soft-tissue injury requiring inpatient care.

4 – Severe

  • Extensive/deep burns with major systemic risk or substantial tissue loss.

5 – Critical

  • Life-threatening burns/soft-tissue injury with high risk of death without urgent critical care.
Uncertainty rule: if unsure, choose the higher band or escalate.

Important (read before use):
ISS-Lite is a pragmatic bedside guide only. It provides a simplified approximation of injury burden using clinician-selected severity bands and does not constitute formal Injury Severity Score (ISS) or Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) coding.

The output must not be used for trauma registry submission, audit reporting, contractual metrics, or as a substitute for specialist clinical judgement. It does not incorporate physiology (e.g. GCS, blood pressure, lactate), mechanism, frailty, age, anticoagulation, comorbidity, or evolving clinical course.

Note: additional spine dropdowns improve usability, but ISS-Lite calculation still uses a 6-region grouped model (not 9).
